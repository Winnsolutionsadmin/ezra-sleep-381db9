Three things that change the plan

1 · The supplement he already takes is the top safety item

He is on 1,000 mg/day. LiverTox score B, zero adolescent trials, thyroid-active, and no regulator has set a safe level. The run's #1 safety item — a pediatrician conversation, not a block. If it stops mid-baseline, the baseline restarts.

2 · “His clock is shifted” is a hypothesis, not a premise

Delayed sleep phase is a diagnosis of exclusion with a 1–8% base rate in adolescents, and more than 40% of people who meet its criteria do not actually have a delayed melatonin onset. The 14-night baseline answers it: does he sleep well and long on a self-selected schedule while the lateness persists?

3 · Change wake time, not bedtime — and measure the right thing

Every schedule gain in the evidence came from moving wake time with bedtimes unmoved. And no ring has validated sleep-onset latency in adolescents, so the experiment scores onset clock time and total sleep, with a minimum effect of 20 minutes before anything counts.

Ranked: what to actually do

1
B · gooddirect teen evidence

Caffeine before 14:00 only (nothing after)

In 98 teens aged 11–17, every after-6pm caffeinated drink tracked 28 minutes less sleep and 17 minutes later onset. Morning drinks tracked nothing. Timing beats total dose.

Timing Cutoff at 14:00 daily; screen the pre-workout label before any M/W/F session.
What Arya says“This week: nothing with caffeine after 2pm - that includes energy drinks and pre-workout.”
2
A · strongdirect teen evidence

Dim the evening: room light down after 21:00

Ordinary room light (under 200 lux) delayed melatonin onset in 99% of people. Extending evening light flipped a +2 h morning-light advance into a net delay. If you run one light lever, run this one.

Timing From 21:00 until sleep, nightly including weekends.
What Arya says“This week: after 9pm, overhead lights off - one lamp is enough for whatever you're doing.”
3
B · gooddirect teen evidence

Protect a 9-hour sleep opportunity

The only item with unambiguous guideline backing (AAP/AASM: 8–10 h for ages 13–18). Restricting opportunity cancelled the morning-light effect on its own.

Timing Nightly, weekends included; anchor the window to the school-day alarm.
What Arya says“This week: nine hours between lights-out and your alarm, even on nights you're not tired.”
4
B · gooddirect teen evidence

Fixed wake time, seven days a week

The operative variable is wake time, not bedtime. Both school-start experiments gained sleep from a later wake with bedtimes essentially unmoved. 'Consistent bedtime' advice is contradicted.

Timing Every day, including weekends and school holidays.
What Arya says“This week: same wake-up time every single day, Saturday and Sunday included.”
5
B · goodextrapolated teen evidence

Finish lifting at least 90 minutes before bed

Evening exercise is fine — pooled single-session onset effect −0.06 min, and 153,154 wearable nights agree. Only vigorous work ending within about an hour of bed carries a penalty.

Timing M/W/F training days; no constraint on non-training evenings.
What Arya says“This week: finish your Tonal session at least 90 minutes before you want to be asleep.”
6
B · gooddirect teen evidence

Daylight immediately after waking

90 minutes at ~8,600 lux right after waking advanced phase by +2.0 ± 1.1 h. Run it last: evening light control dominates it and can cancel it outright.

Timing Start within ~5-15 min of habitual wake. Anchor to HABITUAL wake, not to a target wake - a light pulse landing before the core-temperature minimum DELAYS rather than advances (a pulse beginning ~7 h after DLMO still produced a 0.94 h delay; advances began only ~2 h later).
What Arya says“This week: get outside for 20 minutes right after you get up, before anything else.”
7
C · thindirect teen evidence

Phone out of reach after lights-out

Only after-bedtime use carries signal (r = −0.09), and it is small. The cleanest RCT — two weeks of full device removal at 97% compliance — found nothing on EEG. Worth a block, not a crusade.

Timing From lights-out until the morning alarm.
What Arya says“This week: your phone charges outside your room overnight.”
8
C · thinextrapolated teen evidence

Warm shower or bath 1-2 hours before bed

The thermoregulatory mechanism is real (core temperature falling ~2 h before onset). The famous 'falls asleep 10 minutes faster' number traces to a dead press release.

Timing 60-120 min before intended sleep.
What Arya says“This week: hot shower about an hour and a half before you want to be asleep.”

C · thinnone teen evidence

Reduce bedroom noise exposure

Largest environmental effect in the evidence base (~20 dB equivalent) — but in 5,592 adults, cross-sectional, and structural rather than something you do weekly.

Timing Structural / one-time.

Before any supplement conversation: the harm gate

Screen and route first. These are the referral triggers that stop the experiment and start a phone call:

  • Snoring, gasps, pauses, labored breathing → sleep study. Symptom report cannot substitute.
  • Restless legs → ferritin below 50 ng/mL is the threshold; testing and iron are clinician-supervised.
  • Mood / anxiety → the 4-item NIMH ASQ takes 20 seconds; any single “yes” is a positive screen.
  • Assess sleep before any ADHD stimulant, not after.
  • Total sleep falling below baseline for 3+ nights under any block; new daytime sleepiness the schedule doesn't explain; any liver symptom while ashwagandha is still on board.

How the experiment runs

Instruments: Oura ring + Withings under-mattress mat, one row per night. Baseline: 14 sensor-only nights, no asks. Blocks: one lever at a time, 7–14 nights each, the prediction written down the day before, scored by code afterward. Order: evening light and caffeine first (they dominate), fixed wake time, then morning daylight last. Arya: answers him like a friend; one opt-in morning text (“hours + 1–5”); mentions this week's one thing only if he asks; any “stop” ends it.
